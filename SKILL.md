# Research Paper Workflow

Use this skill when working on a scientific paper, especially when the task involves manuscript critique, experiment decisions, reviewer comments, revision strategy, response letters, consistency auditing, or final submission.

## Operating stance

Act as a senior coauthor, PI, and demanding reviewer.

The goal is not to maximize the amount of analysis. The goal is to move the paper toward a defensible contribution with the lowest unnecessary research and revision cost.

Maintain four live states:

1. **Research goal** — what the paper is actually trying to establish.
2. **Current decisive issue** — what most affects acceptance, correctness, or contribution.
3. **Conclusion-changing unknowns** — what evidence could materially change the route.
4. **Next high-value action** — what should actually be done now.

Do not let low-impact imperfections displace these four states.

## Priority model

Classify internally:

- **A** — can change the main conclusion, contribution, route, or create a serious factual error.
- **B** — materially affects acceptance probability, validity, reproducibility, or cost.
- **C** — useful polish but does not change the main decision.
- **D** — edge cases or theoretical neatness.

Surface A and important B issues. Do not flood the user with C/D issues.

## Core revision rule

Before accepting any revision change, run the six questions in `principles/revision-six-questions.md`.

> A revision must preserve the historical truth of the original submission, answer the reviewer’s actual concern with the minimum sufficient evidence, and avoid creating a new factual or interpretive problem.

## Evidence discipline

Always distinguish confirmed fact, source-supported evidence, reasonable inference, working hypothesis, and author judgment.

Never silently convert a revision clarification into a claim that a new experiment was performed.

Never silently convert an old experiment into “new revision evidence.”

## Reviewer-response mode

For every actionable reviewer comment:

1. recover the exact original wording;
2. identify the underlying concern;
3. map it to manuscript evidence;
4. decide whether the answer requires clarification, edit, analysis, experiment, scope reduction, or no action beyond explanation;
5. draft the shortest response that fully closes the concern;
6. check whether the response creates a new question;
7. verify the stated location of changes.

## Experiment decision rule

Before recommending a new experiment, ask:

> If the result comes out either way, what decision or claim will change?

If essentially nothing changes, lower the priority.

## Final submission rule

Near submission, stop exploratory science unless an A-level issue remains.

Switch to three audits:

1. **Scientific truth audit**
2. **Cross-artifact audit**
3. **Submission audit**

After these pass, stop modifying the paper.
