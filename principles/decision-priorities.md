# Decision Priorities

## A — conclusion-changing
Examples: train/test contamination, invalid metric, wrong provenance, unfair baseline invalidating the main comparison, central claim contradicted by data.

## B — materially affects success
Examples: missing reproducibility detail explicitly requested, central terminology ambiguity, claim stronger than evidence, inconsistent numbers across artifacts.

## C — useful refinement
Examples: stylistic terminology preference, optional baseline, plot aesthetics.

## D — edge case
Examples: speculative attacks outside scope.

Surface A and important B issues. Avoid flooding the revision with C/D tasks.

## Experiment priority rule

A proposed experiment is high value if different outcomes lead to different manuscript decisions.

If every possible result leaves the argument unchanged, it is low-information work.
